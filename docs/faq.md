# Lords of the Lowlands: Frequently Asked Questions

One hundred short answers to the questions new and intermediate lords ask most. For the full picture, read the [User Manual](manual.md).

## Getting started

### 1. What kind of game is Lords of the Lowlands?
It is a real-time strategy game in your browser. You gather resources, build a settlement, raise an army and defeat your rivals across seventeen ages, from the Dawn Age camp fire to the Interstellar Age.

### 2. How do I win a game?
That depends on the objective shown at the start: **Conquest** (be the last team standing), **Survive** (hold out for a set number of minutes) or **Advance** (be the first to reach the level's final age). In every mode you also win if all your opponents are defeated.

### 3. When exactly is a player defeated?
When they have no land units left and no buildings other than houses and farms. Ships alone do not keep a player alive, and once a player is defeated, everything they still own disappears.

### 4. Where should a new player start?
Start with campaign chapter 1, *The First Fire*: a small Dawn Age map against one easy opponent. If it feels too fast, choose **Super easy** in the campaign screen's *Difficulty* box before clicking the chapter.

### 5. Where can I see all the keyboard shortcuts?
Press `?` or `F1` in a game, or click the **?** button in the top bar. Every command button also shows its hotkey in its corner.

### 6. Can I read this manual and FAQ inside the game?
Yes. Use **Manual & FAQ** in the main menu or on the sign-in screen, or press `F4` during a game (it is also in the game menu). The window has a search box.

### 7. How is my score calculated?
Gathered resources ÷ 10, plus the value of your kills ÷ 5, plus 60 per technology, 400 per age advanced, 15 per building and 5 per unit trained. A winner gets 2,000 more plus a speed bonus of 1,500 − 30 per minute played (never below 0).

## Accounts and saving

### 8. What is the difference between a guest and a registered account?
Guests can play everything. Registered players can also save and load games, appear in the Hall of Fame, have their campaign progress recorded, publish levels from the editor and protect their account with two-factor sign-in.

### 9. What name and password can I choose?
Names are 3–20 characters long and may use letters, digits, `_` and `-`. Passwords need at least 8 characters by default (the server operator may require more).

### 10. Why does the Register tab say "Request account", or why is it missing?
The server operator decides the account policy. *Request account* means an administrator has to approve your account before you can sign in. A missing tab means registration is closed. A missing Guest tab means guest play is turned off.

### 11. How do I save a game?
As a registered player, open the game menu (`F10`) and choose **Save Game**, or press `Ctrl+S` for a quick save named after the level and game time. Guests cannot save.

### 12. How do I continue a saved game?
Open **Load Game** in the main menu and click **Resume**. The game reopens exactly as saved in a new battle room: your seat is restored, other human seats are open for anyone to join, and seats nobody takes are played by the computer.

### 13. How many games can I save?
Up to 20 per player by default. The server operator can change this limit. Delete old saves from the **Load Game** screen.

### 14. Does the game pause while I am in the menu?
No. The game keeps running while the game menu, the tech tree or the help window is open, so close them quickly when you are under attack.

### 15. How do I turn on two-factor sign-in (MFA)?
Go to **Settings**, then *Account security*, and click **Enable authenticator (MFA)**. Add the key or setup link to an authenticator app, then enter its 6-digit code. From then on you sign in with your password plus the current code.

## Controls and interface

### 16. How do I select units?
Left-click a unit, or drag a box around several of your units. Hold `Shift` to add units to the selection or remove them, and double-click a unit to select all visible units of the same type.

### 17. How do I give orders?
Right-click. Depending on the target, your units move, gather, attack, build or repair. With only buildings selected, a right-click sets their rally point.

### 18. What is attack-move, and when should I use it?
Press `A` (or the *Attack Move* button), then click a destination. Your units walk there and fight every enemy they meet on the way. Use it whenever you advance into enemy territory; a plain move order walks past enemies.

### 19. How do control groups work?
Select units and press `Ctrl+1` to `Ctrl+9` to store them as a group. `Shift+number` adds the current selection to a group. Press the number to select the group, and press it twice quickly to centre the camera on it.

### 20. How do I find idle workers and soldiers?
Press `.` to jump to the next idle worker and `,` to the next idle soldier. `Ctrl+.` and `Ctrl+,` select all idle workers or all idle soldiers at once.

### 21. How do I quickly select my town centre or my army?
`H` selects and centres on your town centre (`Shift+H` cycles through several). `M` selects all military units and `Ctrl+A` selects all your units.

### 22. How do I move the camera and zoom?
Use the arrow keys, move the mouse to a screen edge, or click and drag on the minimap. The mouse wheel or `+` and `−` zoom, and `0` resets the zoom. `Backspace` centres on your selection.

### 23. What does Space do?
It jumps the camera to your latest attack alert. If there was none recently, the game tells you so.

### 24. How do I build a building quickly?
With workers selected, press `Shift` plus the building's letter: `E` house, `G` granary, `L` lumber camp, `N` mining camp, `F` farm, `D` dock, `C` town centre, `P` generator, `B` barracks, `R` range, `S` stable, `K` workshop, `Y` academy, `T` tower. Then click to place it.

### 25. Can I place several buildings in a row?
Yes. Hold `Shift` while clicking to place one building after another, such as a row of houses or farms. Right-click or `Esc` stops placing.

### 26. How do I train five units at once?
Hold `Shift` while clicking a train button or pressing its hotkey. A building's queue holds up to 10 items.

### 27. How do I cancel something in a production queue?
Select the building and click the item in its queue. You get its full cost back. Cancelling a building under construction refunds 75 %.

### 28. What do the colours in the tech tree mean?
Each cell is marked **Done**, **Available now**, **In progress**, **Later age** or **Not on this map**. Open the tech tree with `F2`, `Y` or the *Tech Tree* button, and click an available technology to research it at your least busy academy.

### 29. How do I chat during a game?
Press `Enter`, type your message and press `Enter` again. `Tab` switches between **All** (everyone in the game), **Team** (allies only) and **Global** (everyone on the server).

## Economy

### 30. What are the five resources?
Food, wood, stone, gold and energy. Gold is only used from the Copper Age on (earlier prices are charged in food instead), and energy only from the Industrial Age.

### 31. How do workers gather?
Select workers and right-click a resource. They work until their hands are full, carry the load to the nearest drop-off building for that resource and return. A worker carries 10 plus the age number per trip.

### 32. Which buildings accept which resources?
The town centre takes food, wood, stone and gold. The granary takes food, the lumber camp wood, and the mining camp stone, gold and energy crystals. Fish can only be delivered to a dock.

### 33. Why are my workers so slow at bringing resources in?
They are probably walking too far. Build a lumber camp at the forest edge, a mining camp right next to the mines and a granary beside the berries or farms. Drop-off camps cost only 80 wood.

### 34. What happens if I switch a worker to a different resource while it carries something?
It first delivers what it is carrying, then goes to the new resource. If there is no drop-off for its old load, the load is lost.

### 35. My workers keep bumping into each other. Is that a problem?
No. Workers walking along a path pass through each other, so busy forest lanes and crowded drop-offs do not jam. Only standing units step aside.

### 36. Which food source is the fastest?
Hunting. A killed animal's carcass is gathered 1.3 times as fast as wood. Fish come next (1.1), then berries (0.9) and farms (0.8). Deer give 120 food, boars 260 and mammoths 650.

### 37. Why did my worker die while hunting?
Wild boars and mammoths fight back. Hunt them with four or more workers or a few soldiers. Deer simply run away.

### 38. How do farms work?
A farm costs 60 wood, is worked by one worker and holds 320 food in the Dawn Age (25 % more per age). When the food is used up the farm disappears and you build a new one. A worker that finishes a farm starts farming it immediately.

### 39. Why do my farms give less food on some maps?
Farms yield only 60 % on desert maps and 115 % on riverland maps. Irrigation (+50 %), Qanats (+40 %) and Terrace Farming (+25 %) raise the yield of farms built after the research.

### 40. How does population work?
Each town centre gives 10 population and each house 5, up to a maximum of 200. Workers, infantry, ranged units and fishing boats take 1, mounted units and warships 2, and siege engines 3.

### 41. Why doesn't my unit start training?
You are at your population limit: a queued unit waits until there is room. Build houses (30 wood) a little before you need them. The population counter is highlighted when you are within 2 of the limit.

### 42. How do I get energy?
From the Industrial Age, build **generators** (120 wood, 80 stone, 40 gold), which produce energy all the time, and mine **energy crystals** with workers, delivering them to a mining camp. Generators produce more in later ages and with gathering technologies.

### 43. How many workers should I have?
A good guide is what the computer aims for: 14 + 3 × your age number, up to 45. That is about 14 in the Dawn Age, 26 in the Iron Age and 44 in the Information Age. Never let your town centre stand idle in the early game.

## Ages and technology

### 44. How do I advance to the next age?
Select your town centre (`H`) and click **Advance to …**. The full cost is paid up front, and the advance takes 40 to 95 seconds depending on the age.

### 45. What does advancing cost?
The Neolithic Age costs 500 food, the Copper Age 800 food and 200 wood, and the Bronze Age 1,000 food, 300 wood, 150 stone and 100 gold. Costs rise with every age, up to 3,600 food, 1,300 wood, 800 stone, 1,400 gold and 1,500 energy for the Interstellar Age. From the Machine Age every advance also costs energy.

### 46. Can I skip ages or advance in several places at once?
No. You advance one age at a time, and only one advance can be in progress. While advancing, that town centre trains no workers, so a second town centre is useful.

### 47. Why is there no "Advance" button?
You have reached the level's final age, or an advance is already queued. Every level has a start age and a final age that you cannot go beyond.

### 48. What happens to my old units when I advance?
They keep their old stats; they are not upgraded. From then on, every building trains the new unit of your current age, which is stronger.

### 49. Where do I research technologies?
At the **academy**, which you can build from the Neolithic Age (175 wood, 50 stone). Its buttons show the next technology of each line, or you can click a technology in the tech tree (`F2`). Several academies can research at the same time.

### 50. What do the four technology lines do?
**Gathering** gives +15 % gathering speed (also for generators). **Attack** gives +10 % attack for military units and buildings. **Armour** gives +5 % armour for military units. **Hit points** gives +10 % hit points for all your units and buildings, including existing ones. Every technology also makes workers 3 % faster.

### 51. Did I miss the Dawn Age technologies because there is no academy yet?
No. Every technology stays available from its age onward, so you can research the Dawn Age ones as soon as your first academy stands in the Neolithic Age.

### 52. What are map technologies?
Eight extra technologies that only exist on certain map types: Irrigation, Terrace Farming, Fishing Nets, Deep Mining, Navigation, Caravans, Qanats and Steam Ships. The tech tree marks the ones your map does not offer as "Not on this map".

## Military and combat

### 53. What are the unit lines?
Workers (town centre), infantry (barracks), ranged units (range), mounted units, later tanks and fighters (stable, from the Copper Age), and siege engines (workshop, from the Bronze Age). Naval maps add fishing boats and warships (dock).

### 54. Which unit beats which?
Infantry beat mounted units (×1.6), mounted units beat ranged units (×1.6), ranged units beat infantry (×1.4), and siege beats buildings (×3.5). Mounted units also crush siege engines (×1.8).

### 55. Why do my archers do almost nothing to buildings?
Ranged units deal only 40 % damage to buildings, and buildings absorb half of every hit with their armour. Use siege engines (×3.5) or, early on, infantry and mounted units.

### 56. Why do my catapults run away from enemies?
Siege engines from the Catapult onward have a minimum range of 2 tiles and back off when enemies come too close. Escort them with infantry or mounted units. The Battering Ram fights in melee instead.

### 57. What is area (splash) damage?
Grenades, catapults, cannon, tanks, artillery, missiles, dreadnoughts and later warships deal half damage to every enemy near the impact point. Spread your units out against them and avoid huge blobs.

### 58. How does armour work?
Armour absorbs a share of every hit: infantry 10 %, mounted 15 %, siege 25 %, buildings 50 %. Each armour technology adds 5 % for your military units, up to 80 % in total.

### 59. How do rally points work?
Select a production building and right-click the ground. Newly trained units walk to that spot, which is handy for gathering an army near the front.

### 60. How do towers and the town centre defend?
Towers (from the Neolithic Age, 40 wood and 120 stone) shoot enemies within 7 tiles. The town centre shoots too, from the Neolithic Age. Both get stronger with every age and with attack technologies, so fight near them when you defend.

### 61. When do flying units appear?
The Drone Carrier (Orbital Age), the Starfighter and Bombard Frigate (Interplanetary) and the Void Interceptor and Void Dreadnought (Interstellar). They fly straight over water, mountains and forests and cannot be blocked, but every unit can still shoot them.

### 62. When do infantry start shooting?
From the Imperial Age. The Grenadier and all later infantry attack from 3.5 tiles away. Before that, infantry fight in melee.

### 63. How do I repair a building?
Select workers and right-click your damaged building. Repairing is free and takes only time.

### 64. Can I delete my own units or buildings?
Yes. Select them and press `Del` (or use the *Delete* button). Units are removed at once; buildings ask for confirmation first.

## Maps and modes

### 65. What map types are there?
Inland, coastal, riverland, lakes, desert and highland. They change the terrain, the farm yield, whether boats are possible and which map technologies you can research.

### 66. On which maps can I build boats?
On maps with enough water: coastal, riverland and lakes maps, and inland or highland maps that have enough water (though inland maps have no fish). Desert maps and the Moon, Mars and space themes never have boats.

### 67. Why can't I place my dock?
A dock needs the Neolithic Age, a map with boats, and a spot whose edge touches water on at least two tiles. If the dock button is missing completely, the map has no naval play.

### 68. What do fishing boats and warships do?
Fishing boats catch fish and bring it to a dock. Warships shoot from 5.5 tiles away, deal extra damage to buildings (×1.3) and boats (×1.2), and get stronger with Navigation and Steam Ships. Only fishing boats can harvest fish.

### 69. Which map sizes can I choose?
Mini (48×48), Tiny (64), Small (80), Normal (112), Large (144), Very large (176), Huge (208) and Enormous (256). You choose the size in the campaign screen, in skirmish and when creating a lobby battle. Hand-drawn maps keep their own size.

### 70. What can I set in a skirmish?
Map (random, a campaign map or a player-made map), theme, map type, map size, amount of water, start age, final age, number of opponents (1, 2, 3, 5 or 7) and difficulty. Random-map skirmishes are always conquest games.

### 71. What is the demo that starts when I leave the menu alone?
After a few idle minutes on the menu (3 by default), the game shows a self-playing match between computer opponents with an automatic camera. Press any key or click to return.

## Campaign

### 72. How many campaign chapters are there?
Seventeen, from *The First Fire* in the Dawn Age to *Battle for the Stars* in the Interstellar Age. Each chapter starts in one age and lets you advance to the next.

### 73. Do I have to play the chapters in order?
No. Every chapter can be played at any time. Playing them in order is a good way to learn, because each one introduces the next age.

### 74. How do I progress to the next level?
Win a chapter, then pick the next card on the campaign screen. For registered players the highest chapter won is recorded (shown as *Campaign N/17*) and won chapters are marked. Each chapter starts fresh with its own resources, so nothing carries over.

### 75. Can I change the difficulty or map size of a chapter?
Yes. Before clicking a chapter, choose a *Difficulty* (or "Level default") and a *Map size* (or "Chapter default") at the bottom of the campaign screen.

### 76. Which chapters are not about conquest?
Chapter 2, *Valley of Plenty*, is a race to the Copper Age. Chapter 4, *The Sea Peoples*, means surviving 15 minutes, and chapter 12, *No Man's Land*, surviving 20 minutes. All others are conquest.

### 77. Are the campaign opponents allied with each other?
Yes. In the campaign, and in every quick game against the computer, all computer opponents are on one team against you.

## Multiplayer

### 78. How do I create a multiplayer battle?
Open **Multiplayer**, choose a map (random, a campaign map or a player-made map), optionally a map size, type a name and click **Create**. You become the host and set up the seats in the battle room.

### 79. How do I join a battle?
Click **Join** next to a battle in the lobby list. You can only join battles that have not started and still have an open seat.

### 80. How do teams work?
Each seat has a team number from 1 to 8. Players with the same number are allies and share vision. A battle needs at least two players and at least two different teams to start.

### 81. Can I add computer players to a multiplayer battle?
Yes. As host, set a seat to **Computer** and choose its difficulty. You can mix humans and computers freely on any team, and set unused seats to **Closed**.

### 82. How do I whisper to another player?
In the lobby chat, type `/w name message`, or click a name in the *Lords online* list to start a whisper. The lobby chat itself goes to everyone; the battle room and the game have All and Team channels.

### 83. What happens if my connection drops during a battle?
Your seat is kept. Reload the page and sign in again, and you are put straight back into the running game.

### 84. Does leaving a battle count as a loss?
Yes. Choosing *Leave to Menu* during a running battle resigns your seat, and *Resign* in the game menu ends your game immediately. Only an unintended disconnect keeps your seat.

## Computer opponents and difficulty

### 85. What difficulty levels are there?
Six: Super easy, Easy, Normal, Hard, Super hard and Insane. The computer's economy, building and training speed is 55 %, 75 %, 100 %, 130 %, 160 % and 200 % respectively.

### 86. When does the computer attack for the first time?
Not before minute 12 on Super easy, 7 on Easy, 4 on Normal, 3.5 on Hard, 3 on Super hard and 2.5 on Insane. It also needs an army of a certain size first, for example 10 + half its age number on Normal.

### 87. Does the computer cheat?
It plays by your rules: it pays for everything and obeys the population cap. Only Super hard and Insane get free resources (0.3 and 0.8 of each per second), and only Insane sees the whole map.

### 88. How does the computer attack?
In waves. It gathers its army about half-way to you, then strikes; when a wave is spent it retreats to regroup, and each new wave is two units bigger. Once every enemy town centre has fallen, all its soldiers join in to finish the game.

### 89. Will a long game against the computer ever end?
Yes. After 40 minutes the computer attacks with ever smaller armies, so games do not stall. It also rebuilds its town centre if it loses it, so hunt down its last workers.

## Strategy

### 90. Should I attack early or build up my economy?
Both can work. An early attack (rush) is strongest on small maps and against easy opponents. Building up (boom) is strongest on large maps. Most winning games defend the first waves cheaply, boom while the enemy regroups, and then attack with a modern, mixed army.

### 91. When is the right moment to advance?
When you are safe: right after you have beaten an attack, or when your army and towers can hold the next wave. Do not leave your barracks empty to pay for an advance.

### 92. How do I deal with enemy towers?
Bring siege engines, which deal ×3.5 against buildings and outrange towers from the Catapult onward. Without siege, attack with infantry or mounted units. Ranged units are poor against buildings.

### 93. How do I balance my economy across the ages?
Focus on food and wood early. Put a few workers on stone and gold from the Copper Age, because every advance needs them. Move workers to gold from the Renaissance, when armies are paid mostly in gold, and build generators from the Industrial Age.

### 94. How do I win a Survive mission?
Stay compact near your town centre, build towers, keep production running and use counters. You do not need to destroy the enemy, only to still be alive when the timer runs out.

### 95. How do I win an Advance mission?
Treat it as a race. Train workers non-stop, research gathering technologies, gather exactly what the advance costs and defend only as much as needed. Any player reaching the final age first wins for their team, computers included.

### 96. Why did my conquest not end even though the enemy base is destroyed?
The enemy still has a land unit or a building other than a house or farm somewhere: a hidden worker, an outlying camp or a tower. Search the map, and remember that the enemy's remaining ships do not count.

## Technical and troubleshooting

### 97. Why is there no sound?
Check the volume sliders and *Mute all* in **Settings**, and press `Shift+M` in a game to toggle mute. Browsers only start sound after your first click or key press.

### 98. Where are my sound settings stored?
In your browser. They are kept the next time you play on the same browser, but not on other devices.

### 99. Why was I removed from a game with a warning about cheating?
The server checks every command. Players who appear to see through the fog of war, send impossible commands or act at inhuman speed are warned, removed and reported, and their results are not recorded. Play with the normal game client only.

### 100. The game says "Cannot build there". Why?
The spot is on water, a mountain or a ford, is not explored yet, or overlaps another building, a tree or a mine. Move the ghost until it shows as valid.
